Timeline of Theories

Theory Year Proposed Who Proposed It Description
Dark matter from unseen stars 1884 Lord Kelvin Suggested that invisible stars could contribute to the galaxy's mass.
Missing mass in the universe 1932 Henri Poincaré Poincaré discussed the possibility of dark matter, using the term matière obscure. He considered whether invisible matter could help explain the motion of stars
Dark matter in galaxy clusters 1933 Fritz Zwicky Zwicky proposed that large amounts of unseen matter existed in the Coma Cluster. He found that galaxies were moving too quickly to be held together by the gravity of visible matter alone.
Modified Newtonian Dynamics (MOND 1983 Mordehai Milgrom Milgrom proposed modifying Newton's laws of motion at extremely low accelerations. Rather than requiring dark matter to explain the rotation of galaxies, MOND suggests that gravity behaves differently on galactic scales.
Weakly Interacting Massive Particles (WIMPs) 1980s No one source WIMPs became a major dark matter hypothesis. The theory proposes that dark matter consists of relatively massive particles that interact very weakly with ordinary matter, making them difficult to detect.
Axion dark matter 1983 John Preskill, Mark Wise, and Frank Wilczek The researchers showed that hypothetical axions, originally proposed to address a problem in particle physics, could also account for dark matter. Axions would be extremely light particles that interact very weakly with ordinary matter.
MACHO hypothesis 1980s Various Massive Compact Halo Objects (MACHOs) were proposed as possible sources of dark matter. These could include faint stars, brown dwarfs and compact objects. Observations later showed that MACHOs could not account for all dark matter.
Cold Dark Matter 1980s Various This theory proposes that dark matter consists of slow-moving, non-ordinary particles. Because these particles move relatively slowly, gravity can gather them into structures that help explain the formation of galaxies and galaxy clusters..
